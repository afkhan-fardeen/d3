# Fingerprint Attendance System: Strengths, Limits and Setup

A fingerprint attendance system lets employees clock in and out by placing a finger on a scanner. It is the most common biometric attendance method because the devices are affordable, quick and proven. It also has limits, especially for workers whose fingers are worn, and you should know them before you buy.

## How it works

The scanner reads the ridges of a finger and converts them into a template. When an employee clocks in, the device compares the new scan with stored templates and logs the match with a time stamp. Most devices keep logs locally and sync them to attendance software over the network. `[CONFIRM TimeTech device behaviour]`

## Strengths

- **Stops buddy punching.** A finger cannot be lent to a colleague like a card.
- **Fast.** A scan takes about a second on a good device.
- **Low running cost.** There are no cards to replace.
- **Proven.** Fingerprint is a mature technology with many device options.
- **Works offline.** Devices store logs until they reconnect.

## Limits to plan for

| Problem | Cause | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Failed reads | Dry, cut or worn fingers, common in construction, cleaning and kitchen work | Enrol two or three fingers, pick a quality sensor, or add face or card |
| Slow queues at shift change | Many staff, one device | Install more devices or use face recognition |
| Hygiene concerns | Shared touch surface | Use face recognition or provide sanitiser and cleaning routines |
| Dust and moisture | Industrial sites | Choose rated hardware and keep the sensor clean |
| Privacy worries | Fingerprints are sensitive data | Explain use, store templates only, restrict access |

## Optical or capacitive sensors

- **Optical** sensors photograph the fingerprint. They are cheaper but more sensitive to dirt and wear.
- **Capacitive** sensors read the electrical pattern of the skin. They are usually more accurate and harder to fool. `[CONFIRM which sensors TimeTech devices use]`

## Setup in seven steps

1. **Plan device locations.** Place devices at entrances where staff queue, at a height people can reach, away from glare and dust.
2. **Choose the number of devices.** A rule of thumb is one device for each group of staff arriving at the same time; test it at your busiest shift.
3. **Connect to the network.** Give each device a fixed address, or use a cloud connection.
4. **Set up the software.** Create departments, shifts, grace periods and overtime rules.
5. **Enrol employees.** Scan at least two fingers per person and test immediately. Enrol staff with difficult fingers separately.
6. **Run a pilot.** Run it alongside your old method for one or two weeks and check for missed punches.
7. **Link to payroll.** Test one pay run with the attendance data before going live.

## Fingerprint, face or card?

| | Fingerprint | Face recognition | Card |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Buddy punching | Prevented | Prevented | Possible |
| Touch required | Yes | No | Yes (tap) |
| Handles worn fingers | Poorly | Yes | Yes |
| Typical cost | Low | Medium | Lowest |
| Best for | Offices, retail | Hospitals, factories, large sites | Low-risk sites |

## FAQ

**How does a fingerprint attendance system work?**
It scans a finger, matches it to a stored template and records the time.

**What if an employee's fingerprint will not scan?**
Enrol more than one finger, clean the sensor, or offer face recognition or a card as a backup.

**Do fingerprint systems store my actual fingerprint?**
Most store a template, not an image. Ask the vendor to confirm. `[CONFIRM]`

**Can it connect to payroll?**
Yes, if the attendance software integrates with your payroll. This removes manual data entry.

**How many fingerprints can a device hold?**
It depends on the model. Check capacity against your headcount with room to grow.
